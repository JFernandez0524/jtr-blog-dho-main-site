When selling an inherited house or closing an estate sale in New Jersey — especially in active real estate markets like Perth Amboy — sellers are often surprised at the closing table when they see a huge line item on their settlement statement: **a Tax Waiver Escrow holding back a significant portion (sometimes most) of their net proceeds.**

If this happened to you or a family member, you might naturally wonder: *What is a tax waiver? Why is the title company holding my money? And when do I get it back?*

Here is a plain-English explanation of how New Jersey tax waivers work, why title companies require escrows, and how we handle them during Perth Amboy real estate sales.

---

## What Is a New Jersey Tax Waiver (Form 0-1)?

Under New Jersey law (**N.J.S.A. 54:35-5**), when a property owner passes away, the State of New Jersey automatically attaches a statutory tax lien to all real estate owned by the decedent at the time of their death.

To clear this lien and transfer marketable title to a new buyer, the State requires an official document called an **Inheritance Tax Waiver (Form 0-1)** issued by the New Jersey Division of Taxation.

The Form 0-1 certificate serves as official proof that either:
1. No New Jersey Inheritance or Estate Tax is owed, or
2. Any taxes owed to the state have been fully paid.

Without this waiver, the title company cannot guarantee clear, unencumbered ownership to the buyer.

---

## Why Is Most of the Sale Price Held in Escrow?

Obtaining a Form 0-1 Tax Waiver directly from the State of New Jersey takes time — often **3 to 6 months or longer**, depending on state processing backlogs and tax return reviews.

If buyers and sellers had to wait for the State of New Jersey to process the tax waiver before closing, real estate transactions would sit in limbo for months.

To keep the sale moving on schedule, New Jersey title companies and closing attorneys use a **Tax Waiver Escrow Agreement**:

1. **The Sale Closes on Schedule:** The buyer receives the keys, and the deed is recorded.
2. **Funds Are Reserved in Escrow:** The title company or closing attorney holds a predetermined amount (often a major portion of the seller's proceeds, or calculated potential tax liability plus a safety buffer) in an escrow account.
3. **The Lien Protection:** The escrow ensures that if the state later determines tax is owed, funds are readily available to pay it without placing a lien on the buyer's new home.

---

## When Do Sellers Get Their Escrowed Money Back?

The escrowed funds **belong to the seller or the estate**. The title company is simply holding them in trust until the state paperwork completes.

Here is the typical timeline:

1. **Post-Closing Submission:** The estate's attorney or accountant submits the final state tax return or affidavit to the NJ Division of Taxation.
2. **State Processing:** The state reviews the filing and issues the official Form 0-1 Tax Waiver.
3. **Escrow Release:** The attorney/title company receives the Form 0-1, clears the file, and immediately dispatches the remaining escrow balance directly to the seller or estate bank account.
