He had already bought his replacement home down in Florida, so every single month this house sat unsold in NJ, he was stuck **paying two mortgages at $4,000 a month out of pocket.**

Frank opened up his phone while we were talking and read off his numbers:
- **900+ views on Zillow**
- **55 saves**
- **5 groups at the weekend open house**
- **A buyer who toured the home TWICE — including coming back a second time during a torrential rainstorm**

He said to me, *"Jose, I’m getting upset. I’ve got nearly 1,000 views, dozens of saves, and buyers coming back twice... but I’ve got zero offers. I don’t care if people hate the house, just tell me why so I can fix it! My agent gives me no feedback and I don't know what buyers are thinking."*

If you are a home seller in New Jersey asking **"Why is my house getting Zillow views but no offers?"**, Frank's situation probably hits close to home. 

Here is the exact real talk I gave Frank about how the market actually works, why online vanity metrics lie to sellers, and how to fix a stale listing before carrying costs eat up your equity.

## 1. Zillow Views and Saves Are "Vanity Metrics" (And Why They Deceive Sellers)

Sellers track Zillow stats religiously. You hit refresh on your listing, see the view count tick past 900, watch the "saves" climb past 50, and watch your **ShowingTime app** notifications ring every time an agent requests a tour.

It feels like momentum. But in real estate sales, **views and saves are vanity metrics.**

Here is why:
- **Views** mean people liked clicking on your listing photo while scrolling through their phone on the couch.
- **Saves** mean people added your home to a wish list or want to keep an eye on it in case you drop the price by $50,000 later.
- **Saves do NOT equal pre-approved buyers willing to sign a purchase contract at your current list price.**

I’ve sold houses in New Jersey that had 3,000 views and 200 saves sit for two months without a single offer. I’ve also listed homes that got 150 views and had 4 competing cash offers by Sunday night. 

Don't let high Zillow views trick you into thinking your pricing strategy is working. If buyers are looking online and stepping foot inside, but nobody is making an offer, the market is sending you a clear message.

---

## 2. How Many Showings Opening Weekend Is Normal in New Jersey?

When you launch a new listing in New Jersey, buyer interest is at its absolute peak during the first 7 to 14 days. 

Here are the real metrics you should measure:

### The 20-to-30 Showings Benchmark
For a competitively priced home in North or Central Jersey (whether in Hunterdon, Middlesex, Monmouth, or Union County), your goal for opening weekend and the first 10 days should be **20 to 30 total showings**. 

If your listing only generates **4 to 8 showings** over your first two weekends, your price is too high to entice buyers off their couches — no matter how many Zillow saves you have.

### The 14-Day Offer Rule
If a home gets 15 to 20+ showings and zero written offers within 14 days, the market is giving you direct feedback: **the property is overpriced relative to its current condition, layout, or nearby competition.**

## 3. Why Open House Feedback & Showing Feedback Matter (And Why Radio Silence Kills Sales)

Frank’s biggest frustration wasn’t just the lack of offers — it was **radio silence from showing agents.** 

He had 9 people walk through his home, plus 5 open house visitors. A couple even came back a second time during a rainstorm and then completely ghosted. Yet, his listing agent couldn't tell him *why* those buyers walked away.

As a seller, you can't take feedback personally. Whether a buyer thinks:
- The kitchen and bathrooms need $50,000 in updates,
- The roof or windows will need replacing down the road, or
- The lot slope or driveway position isn't ideal,

**Your listing agent must aggressively chase down showing feedback from every single agent who opens that lockbox.** Without real feedback, you are flying blind. Non-offers *are* feedback, but detailed comments tell you whether you have a condition problem, a presentation problem, or a pricing problem.

## 4. The Hidden Math: Carrying Costs vs. Holding Out on Price

When sellers are reluctant to lower their asking price, they often say, *"I'd rather wait 3 or 4 months to get my number than sell for less today."*

If you are paying two mortgages — or holding a vacant property — this logic breaks down quickly due to **carrying costs**.

Let's look at Frank's numbers:
- **Carrying Cost:** $4,000 per month (Mortgage PITI + pool maintenance + utilities)
- **Time on Market:** 4 months of waiting = **$16,000 paid out of pocket**

Look at how the math actually plays out:

| Strategy | List Price / Accepted Offer | Months on Market | Total Carrying Costs | Net Proceeds to Seller |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Option A: Hold out for top dollar** | $735,000 offer | 4 Months | -$16,000 | **$719,000** |
| **Option B: Adjust price early to trigger a fast sale** | $715,000 offer | 1 Month | -$4,000 | **$711,000** |

Notice that after accounting for $16,000 in lost mortgage payments, holding out for 4 months to get a $20,000 higher price **only netted the seller an extra $8,000** — while taking on months of stress, risk, and additional property tax exposure. 

Bleeding out $4,000 every 30 days is cash you never get back.

## 5. Strategic Pricing: Why Pricing 5%–10% Below Target Value Drives Bidding Wars

Many sellers ask: *"Should I lower my home price to $699k to get multiple offers?"*

When you price a home at the absolute top of the market (say, $740,000), buyers look at it and compare it to fully updated homes in the neighborhood. They see the original bathrooms or older roof and walk away. You end up with 4 showings and zero offers.

**The Strategic Pricing Formula:** Price 5% to 10% *under* your target market value to ignite buyer demand.

Instead of listing at $740,000:
1. List the property at **$699,000**.
2. Suddenly, every buyer with a $700k search filter on Zillow or Realtor.com gets an instant alert.
3. Buyers see a 5-acre home with a pool, wet bar, and top-rated school district (like North Hunterdon High School) priced under $700k and think, *"We need to see this home today!"*
4. Instead of 4 showings, you get **25 to 30 showings** in 48 hours.
5. Multiple buyers submit competing offers, creating buyer urgency (FOMO) that drives the final sale price **above $730,000+**.

### A Real NJ Example: 339 Lawrie Street in Perth Amboy
I used this exact strategy on an inherited house at **339 Lawrie Street in Perth Amboy**. The seller originally wanted $380,000, but foot traffic was cold because the house needed cosmetic updates. 

We made a swift, strategic price move down to **$349,000**. 

That single move opened the floodgates. We got over 30 showings in one weekend, packed the open house, generated multiple competitive offers, and closed the sale at **$385,000 — $35,000 over the revised asking price!**

## 6. Stop Making "Half-Assed" $5,000 or $10,000 Price Drops

One of the biggest mistakes listing agents make with a stale listing is dropping the price by $5,000 or $10,000 on a $700,000+ home. 

I call these "half-assed" price cuts. 

A $5,000 price drop does **not** attract a new buyer pool. A buyer who passed on your home at $735,000 is not going to change their mind at $730,000. All a small price drop does is tell the market that you are testing the waters and open to bleeding out slowly.

If you are going to make a price move:
- **Make a bold move.**
- **Cross key price brackets** (e.g., dropping below $700k to $699,000).
- **Give your agent the leverage to negotiate buyers UP** from a position of strength and high demand.

---

## 7. Action Plan: What To Do If Your NJ Home Is Sitting With No Offers

If your listing has been on the market for more than 14 days without an offer, take these steps immediately:

1. **Check Your Showings Count:** If you are under 15 showings, your pricing is blocking buyers from visiting in person.
2. **Demand Feedback:** Tell your listing agent you want written feedback from every single agent who requested a tour through ShowingTime.
3. **Calculate Your Daily Carrying Costs:** Factor in your mortgage interest, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance costs so you know your exact financial drain per day.
4. **Execute a Bold Price Adjustment:** Stop trimming $5k at a time. Reposition the home below psychological search thresholds (like $699k) to generate a flood of new buyers.
